## Why it matters

Objective 6.4 covers the support of solutions in production. The architect selects the Cloud Customer Care service that matches the business risk, sets up the process to open and manage cases, and designs incident response. The exam gives you an outage or a requirement ("15-minute response", "a named Google contact", "is Google down or is it us?") and asks for the correct plan, priority, escalation path, or signal.

## Key points

- Cloud Customer Care has four services: Basic (free for all customers), Standard, Enhanced, and Premium. The support service applies to all projects in the organization ([Get support with Cloud Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/overview)).
- Target initial response for P1 cases is 1 hour with Enhanced Support and 15 minutes with Premium Support. Standard Support has no P1 target. Its fastest target is 4 hours for P2 ([Technical Support Services Guidelines](https://cloud.google.com/terms/tssg)).
- P1 means "Critical Impact—Service Unusable in Production". The example situation also requires a GA feature and no workaround that takes less than 30 minutes ([Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures)).
- Premium Support assigns a Technical Account Manager (TAM). Enhanced Support can add the Technical Account Advisor Service as a Value-Add Service ([Get support with Cloud Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/overview), [Enhanced Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/enhanced)).
- Escalation is for a broken support process. For a high-impact issue, set the correct priority instead. Escalation usually does not make a case go faster ([Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures)).
- Personalized Service Health (PSH) is the primary source for Google Cloud incidents that affect your projects. The public Google Cloud Service Health page shows only broad, severe incidents ([Google Cloud incident communication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/incident-communication)).
- Google's incident process has three main roles: Incident Commander (IC), Communications Lead (CL), and Operations Lead (OL). The CL and the OL report to the IC ([Incident Response](https://sre.google/workbook/incident-response/)).
- A written playbook gives roughly a 3x improvement in mean time to repair (MTTR) compared with "winging it" ([Introduction](https://sre.google/sre-book/introduction/)).
- Put playbook links in the documentation of each alerting policy, so that the notification takes the responder to the steps ([Annotate notifications with user-defined documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/monitoring/alerts/doc-variables)).

### Choose the service from the requirement

| Requirement in the scenario | Recommend |
|---|---|
| Development and test only. Questions are about billing | Basic Support |
| A small team builds a new workload. Business-hours help is enough | Standard Support |
| Production workloads. Critical issues need a response in 1 hour, 24/7 | Enhanced Support |
| Business-critical production. 15-minute P1 response, a TAM, and support that knows your architecture | Premium Support |
| Any disruption of an environment has a significant impact. You need drills, proactive monitoring, and P0 cases | Premium Support with Mission Critical Services (a Value-Add Service, after an assessment) |
| Support personnel must meet location or personnel requirements (for example FedRAMP High, IL4, or CJIS for US organizations) | Assured Support on Enhanced or Premium Support, for Assured Workloads folders |
| A launch or a sale causes a short traffic peak | Event Management Service (Premium Support) or Planned Event Support (Enhanced Support) |

Sources: [Get support with Cloud Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/overview), [Premium Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/premium), [Enhanced Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/enhanced).

Two purchase rules can decide an answer. You need a Google Cloud organization and the Organization Administrator role to buy a support service. Only an Organization Administrator can change it, because it applies to all projects in the organization ([Get support with Cloud Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/overview)).

## Case priority

| Priority | Definition | Example situation |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Critical Impact—Service Unusable in Production | Significant rate of user-facing errors. Critical business impact, such as revenue loss or a possible data integrity issue. No workaround that takes less than 30 minutes. The feature is GA. |
| P2 | High Impact—Service Use Severely Impaired | Production is degraded, or you have difficulty starting a new production system. Moderate business impact. A workaround exists and is quick to apply. |
| P3 | Medium Impact—Service Use Partially Impaired | Limited scope or severity. No user-visible impact. Low business impact. |
| P4 | Low Impact—Service Fully Usable | Little or no impact. Consultative questions that need in-depth analysis. |

Source: [Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures).

Rules for P1 cases:

- Explain in the case why you selected P1 and what the business impact is. A P1 case alerts an expert who works only on your issue and invites you to a live call ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).
- Join the call within 15 to 30 minutes. P1 cases follow the sun by default, so specialists in other regions continue the work 24 hours a day ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).
- Stay available. If you do not respond for more than 3 hours, Customer Care can reduce the case to P2 ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).
- Google reviews the priority that you select and can reclassify it ([Technical Support Services Guidelines](https://cloud.google.com/terms/tssg)).
- A problem in a development version can be P1 if it blocks a critical security fix. Support can reevaluate other P1 cases that do not affect production or cause high business impact ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).

Rules for all cases:

- Check known issues first, and create one case per issue. Customer Care closes duplicate cases ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).
- Give the start time, the product, the zone, the identifiers, and whether the problem is intermittent or consistent ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).
- You can link a case to a known issue to get updates automatically ([Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures)).

## Change the priority or escalate

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| The impact on production increases | Change the priority, for example from P3 to P1. Say how the business impact changed. |
| No update in the agreed time, no progress, or the fix does not work | Escalate. An Escalation Manager owns the escalation, and you get a notice within an hour. |
| A P2 case needs 24-hour attention (Premium Support) | Ask the specialist for follow-the-sun service. |
| The specialist works in a time zone that does not suit you | Ask Customer Care to route the case to your time zone. |
| You have Premium Support and need advice on priority or escalation | Ask your TAM. |

Sources: [Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures), [Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices).

Escalation shortly after a priority change can make the resolution slower ([Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures)).

## Is it Google or is it us? Service Health

During an outage, first find out whether a Google Cloud incident is the cause. Google uses two channels with different scopes.

| Channel | What it shows | Sign-in | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personalized Service Health (PSH): console dashboard, Service Health API, alerts from Cloud Logging, organization views | Incidents relevant to your projects, including limited-scope incidents in one zone, one location, or a subset of projects | Yes | The primary source. Add it to the incident response process as a corroboration signal. |
| Google Cloud Service Health (status.cloud.google.com), with an RSS feed | Broad, severe incidents only | No | A fallback when PSH is not available |

Sources: [Google Cloud incident communication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/incident-communication), [Personalized Service Health overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/overview).

PSH gives each incident a **relevance** for your project: Impacted, Related, Partially Related, Not Impacted, or Unknown. The relevance can change while the incident progresses ([Personalized Service Health concepts](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/concepts)).

Design rules from the docs:

- Do not page on-call engineers for every PSH event. Show PSH incidents on the team's dashboards and tools ([Google Cloud incident communication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/incident-communication)).
- Send alerts only for critical services and locations, or only for events with a relevance of Related or Impacted ([Google Cloud incident communication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/incident-communication)).
- Plan for PSH failure. PSH depends on core services such as IAM. Runbooks must send operators to status.cloud.google.com. Automation uses the Service Health Status API to detect a PSH problem, and then reads the public RSS feed ([Google Cloud incident communication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/incident-communication)).
- Use organization views to see incidents for all projects in one place ([Personalized Service Health overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/overview)).

## Incident management: roles and process

Google's incident management is based on the Incident Command System. It has three goals, the "three Cs": coordinate the response, communicate, and keep control ([Incident Response](https://sre.google/workbook/incident-response/)).

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Incident Commander (IC) | Holds the high-level state of the incident. Assigns roles. Holds every role that is not delegated. Removes roadblocks. |
| Operations Lead (OL) | Applies operational tools to mitigate or resolve the incident. The operations team is the only group that changes the system during the incident. |
| Communications Lead (CL) | The public face of the response. Sends periodic updates to the responders and to stakeholders. Can keep the incident document up to date. |
| Planning | Handles longer-term items: files bugs, arranges handoffs, and records how the system differs from normal, so that you can revert it later. |

Sources: [Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/), [Incident Response](https://sre.google/workbook/incident-response/).

The process rules that the exam tests:

- **Declare early.** It is better to declare an incident and close it quickly than to start the process hours late. Set clear criteria in advance. One Google team declares an incident if a second team must help, if customers can see the outage, or if the issue is not solved after an hour of concentrated analysis ([Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/)).
- **Mitigate first.** Stop the bleeding, restore service, and preserve the evidence for root-cause analysis ([Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/)).
- **Keep a live incident document.** It is the IC's most important responsibility. Keep it for the postmortem ([Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/)).
- **Hand off explicitly.** The outgoing IC does not leave until the new IC confirms the handoff ([Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/)).
- **Open the support case early.** When Google is involved, create the case with time, product, identifiers, and location near the start of your incident process. The person who opened the case is usually the best IC, because that person has the most context ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).

The postmortem process is in [Testing, validation, and root cause analysis](note:4.1-testing-and-troubleshooting). Tracking of postmortem action items is in [Quality control measures](note:6.5-quality-control).

## Runbooks, playbooks, and drills

Prepare before the incident. The SRE workbook recommends these steps ([Incident Response](https://sre.google/workbook/incident-response/)):

1. Train responders on the IC, CL, and OL roles. Encourage a mitigation-first response.
2. Agree on a communication channel, a contact list, and templates for status updates.
3. Write down the criteria for an incident, based on past outages.
4. Run drills. Invent an outage, or recreate one from a postmortem. Use the real tools.

Connect the playbook to the alert. An alerting policy in Cloud Monitoring has documentation with a subject, content, and links. Links can point to playbooks, repositories, and dashboards ([Annotate notifications with user-defined documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/monitoring/alerts/doc-variables)). Use a snooze to stop alerts and notifications for a time period, for example during planned maintenance ([Snooze notifications and alerts](https://docs.cloud.google.com/monitoring/alerts/snooze)).

The operational excellence pillar also says to document incident response procedures in a runbook, and to review and update it regularly ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)). See [The operational excellence pillar](note:6.1-operational-excellence). Alert design is in [Google Cloud Observability](note:6.2-observability).

## Set up support before you need it

| Task | Why |
|---|---|
| Buy the correct support service before production launch | Response targets and 24/7 coverage depend on the service. |
| Grant Tech Support Editor to people who manage cases, and Tech Support Viewer to people who only read them | These roles control who can create, update, escalate, and close cases. |
| Grant Support Account Administrator to the person who manages the support account | This role is needed, for example, to enable case sharing. |
| Configure Essential Contacts for each category (Billing, Legal, Product updates, Security, Suspension, Technical) | Without custom contacts, notifications go to IAM principals as fallback contacts. |
| Create a test support case | The onboarding checklist includes it, so the process works on the day of an outage. |
| Connect the Cloud Support API to your ticket system | A connector can synchronize cases with ServiceNow, Jira Service Desk, or Zendesk. |

Sources: [Onboard to Cloud Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/checklists/onboard-customer-care), [Language support and working hours](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/language-working-hours), [Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures), [Essential Contacts overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/resource-manager/docs/managing-notification-contacts), [Synchronize cases using polling](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/reference/connector).

Two more tools affect support work:

- **Gemini Cloud Assist** (Preview for support cases) can troubleshoot an issue and fill in the case form from the conversation ([Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures)). See [Gemini Cloud Assist](note:1.2-gemini-cloud-assist).
- **Access Transparency and Access Approval.** Access Transparency logs record the actions of Google personnel on your Customer Data. It is a default control for every organization. Access Approval requires your explicit approval before Customer Care or engineering teams can access Customer Data ([Enabling Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/enable), [Overview of Access Approval](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-approval/docs/overview)). See [Organization policy, VPC Service Controls, and audit logging](note:3.1-security-controls).

For a launch or a seasonal peak, the Customer Care checklist covers quotas, reservations, load tests, and DR tests ([Prepare for a peak capacity event](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/checklists/plan-peak-capacity-event)). Capacity and load testing are in [Reliability in production](note:6.6-production-reliability).

## Exam traps

- **Standard Support for production outages.** Standard Support has no P1 target, and it works only during Hours of Operation. A 1-hour P1 target needs Enhanced Support ([Technical Support Services Guidelines](https://cloud.google.com/terms/tssg)).
- **A TAM with Enhanced Support.** A TAM comes with Premium Support. Enhanced Support offers the Technical Account Advisor Service only as a paid Value-Add Service ([Enhanced Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/enhanced)).
- **Escalation to get a faster fix.** Escalation flags a broken process. For more impact, raise the priority ([Create and manage support cases](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/customer-care-procedures)).
- **A second case for the same issue.** Create one case per issue. Customer Care closes duplicates ([Best practices for working with Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/best-practices)).
- **"The status page is green, so Google has no incident."** Limited-scope incidents, such as a single zone, appear only in Personalized Service Health ([Google Cloud incident communication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/incident-communication)).
- **Paging on every PSH event.** The docs recommend dashboards, and alerts filtered to critical services or to Related and Impacted events ([Google Cloud incident communication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/service-health/docs/incident-communication)).
- **Everybody fixes production during an incident.** Only the operations team changes the system. Everyone else keeps to their own role ([Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/)).
- **Root cause before mitigation.** First stop the bleeding and restore service. Preserve the evidence for later analysis ([Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/)).
- **Access Transparency needs a paid support service.** Access Transparency is a default security control for every Google Cloud organization ([Enabling Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/enable)).
