## Why it matters

Objective 4.2 tests how an architect works with people and processes, not only with systems. Questions ask who to involve, what to do first, how to get buy-in, how to close a skills gap, and how to record a decision. Google's answers follow one pattern: assess, find a sponsor and a cross-functional team, start small, prove value with data, train, and measure. An option that fixes a people problem with technology alone is usually wrong.

## Key points

- The Google Cloud Adoption Framework assesses four themes: Learn, Lead, Scale, and Secure ([Migrate to Google Cloud: Get started](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-getting-started)).
- Each theme is in one of three phases: tactical, strategic, or transformational. The themes evaluated in the phases give the Cloud Maturity Scale ([Get started](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-getting-started)).
- Cloud adoption needs top-down mandates from sponsors and bottom-up momentum from cross-functional teams ([Google Cloud Adoption Framework](https://cloud.google.com/adoption-framework)).
- A successful proof of concept (POC) builds stakeholder confidence. It shows tangible benefits to leadership and technical teams ([Best practices for validating a migration plan](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-best-practices)).
- To assess a team, list all skills, the level of expertise for each skill, and the most knowledgeable people. Then find the missing skills ([Optimize your environment](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-optimizing-your-environment)).
- An architecture decision record (ADR) captures the key options, the main requirements, and the decision ([Architecture decision records overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/architecture-decision-records)).
- An error budget is an objective metric that removes the politics from reliability negotiations between SREs and developers ([Embracing risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/)).
- For a change to tools or processes, incremental change is much easier to manage, and early wins help the organization buy into it. For a reorganization, iteration does harm, because frequent org-chart changes reduce employee confidence ([Organizational change management in SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/organizational-change/)).
- Premium Support includes a named Technical Account Manager (TAM), who assesses your cloud maturity and helps create an adoption roadmap ([Premium Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/premium)).
- Disaster recovery (DR) is a subset of business continuity planning ([Disaster recovery planning guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/dr-scenarios-planning-guide)).

## The Google Cloud Adoption Framework

The framework is a map of where your IT capabilities are now and a guide to where you want to be. Use it to evaluate your organization before a migration starts ([Get started](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-getting-started)). It is technology agnostic. You can use it alone or with a Google TAM ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)).

| Theme | What it measures | Epics (workstreams) |
|---|---|---|
| Learn | Learning programs that upskill technical teams, and the use of experienced partners | Upskilling, external experience |
| Lead | The mandate from leadership, and how cross-functional and collaborative the teams are | Sponsorship, teamwork |
| Scale | The use of cloud-native services that reduce operational overhead and automate manual work | Architecture, CI/CD, infrastructure as code |
| Secure | Protection from unauthorized and inappropriate access with a multilayered, identity-centric model | Access, data, and identity management |

Sources: [Google Cloud Adoption Framework](https://cloud.google.com/adoption-framework), [Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf).

| Phase | Typical practice | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Tactical | Individual workloads, but no coherent plan. Little change to people, tools, and operating model. | Quick return on investment (short term) |
| Strategic | A plan develops workloads with future scale in mind. Change reaches people and processes. | Streamline operations (mid term) |
| Transformational | Cloud operations run smoothly. Teams decide more on their own, measured against SLOs. | IT becomes an engine of innovation (long term) |

Sources: [Get started](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-getting-started), [Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf).

To start, the whitepaper gives three steps ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)):

1. Assess your current maturity with your stakeholders, for example with a survey.
2. Decide how far to go. Stakeholders often disagree at first. Tactical objectives can be a stepping stone.
3. Act on each epic where you found a gap. The actions lead to a training program, a change management program, a cloud operating model, and a secure account setup.

The whitepaper is older than some current product names (for example, Stackdriver). Use it for the maturity model, not for product choices.

## Stakeholder management: influencing and facilitation

Identify the stakeholders early, agree on objectives with them, and win their support with evidence. The data transfer guide sorts the people in a project into three groups:

| Group | Who | Their part |
|---|---|---|
| Enable | Storage, IT, and network admins; an executive sponsor; advisors such as the Google account team | Provide the resources |
| Approve | Data owners, legal advisors, a security administrator | Approve against policy and regulation |
| Execute | A team lead, a project manager, an engineering team | Do and track the work |

Source: [Transfer your large datasets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-transferring-your-large-datasets).

Include these owners in planning and decision meetings. Poor organizational planning is often the cause of failed transfer initiatives ([Transfer your large datasets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-transferring-your-large-datasets)).

| Situation in the stem | Google-recommended action |
|---|---|
| Stakeholders have competing visions | Write a vision statement first: the use case, why the current approach fails, and what to optimize. Get sign-off on business and technical objectives. |
| Leaders doubt the value | Run a POC with defined success criteria. Experiments show the value to business leaders. |
| Task ownership is unclear | Add a RACI matrix to the migration plan, next to the technical design document and the timeline. |
| Sponsors sign off and then step back | Sponsors must visibly and continuously support the adoption, and clear roadblocks with other functions. |
| Each audience needs different cost data | Tailor cost reports to the audience. See [cost optimization](note:4.2-cost-optimization). |
| Stakeholders do not trust the delivery team | Deliver small changes often. A proven ability to deploy changes builds trust. |

Sources: [Plan a hybrid and multicloud strategy](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/hybrid-multicloud-patterns/strategy), [Assess and discover your workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-assessing-and-discovering-your-workloads), [Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf), [Foster a culture of cost awareness](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/foster-culture-cost-awareness), [Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework).

**Facilitation.** A workshop brings stakeholders to one decision. For example, a Cloud Sprint from Google Professional Services uses interactive discussions and whiteboarding sessions ([Get started](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-getting-started)).

**A Cloud Center of Excellence (CoE).** At strategic maturity, a dedicated cross-functional team of advocates drives adoption across projects ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)). Successful CoE members are multidisciplinary, so they reflect the stakeholders' views. They are also empowered to decide without higher-level sign-off ([Cloud Center of Excellence](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/training-certifications/optimize-your-organizations-cloud-journey-with-a-cloud-center-of-excellence)).

## Change management

This section covers organizational change: how people and teams change the way they work. Technical change governance (approvals, version control, CI/CD, rollback) is in [operational excellence](note:6.1-operational-excellence).

In the Adoption Framework, three people epics support the change management program ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)):

- **Behaviors.** Find the current and the desired behaviors, such as collaboration, blamelessness, and data-driven decisions. Then plan a change journey.
- **People operations.** Align structure, roles, skills, and performance measures. Reward the new behaviors through incentives. Misaligned goals harm adoption.
- **Communication.** Build a blameless culture. Use postmortems to learn from failures.

The SRE Workbook compares common change management models:

| Model | Core idea | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Lewin's three-stage model | Unfreeze (persuade the group), change, then freeze the new behavior | Macro-level organizational change |
| McKinsey 7-S | Structure, strategy, systems, skills, style, staff, shared values | Organizational effectiveness, such as a move to SRE |
| Kotter's eight-step process | Eight steps to lead a change | Teams that embrace change as a core responsibility |
| Prosci ADKAR | Awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement | People-centric change across distributed teams |
| Bridges Transition Model, Kübler-Ross Change Curve | The emotions of people during change | Managers who support employees |
| Deming cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act) | Continuous improvement of existing processes | Process improvement, not organizational change |

Source: [Organizational change management in SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/organizational-change/).

Google's lessons from its own changes ([Organizational change management in SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/organizational-change/)):

- Grassroots change needs support from executive leadership. A small, focused group from all parts of the organization was key.
- Change tools and processes in increments. Early wins justify more investment. A failed early iteration costs less.
- Keep the cost of migration small relative to its benefits. Otherwise, the migration itself blocks adoption.
- ADKAR calls resistance a "knowledge-to-ability gap". People need time, coaching, and training.

More approval is not the recommended way to make change safe. DORA recommends that you replace heavyweight change-approval processes with peer review ([DevOps capabilities](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/devops)). In the Adoption Framework, manual review by a change advisory board is a tactical practice ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)).

## Team assessment and skills readiness

Assess the skills first. Then close the gaps with training, partners, and hiring:

1. List all skills, the level of expertise, and the most knowledgeable people ([Optimize your environment](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-optimizing-your-environment)).
2. Compare the list with the target design. A new managed service needs the skills to provision, configure, and use it ([Optimize your environment](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-optimizing-your-environment)).
3. Design a training program, or choose one from Google trainers ([Optimize your environment](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-optimizing-your-environment)). Options include public resources, in-depth courses, role-based learning paths, and certifications ([Assess and discover your workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-assessing-and-discovering-your-workloads)).
4. Give the staff a free environment to test and learn. The assessment guide calls this critical ([Assess and discover your workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-assessing-and-discovering-your-workloads)).
5. Use partners, system integrators, or Google Professional Services for the remaining gaps ([Get started](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-getting-started)).
6. Improve the team structure and culture, not only the skills ([Optimize your environment](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-optimizing-your-environment)).

| Learn phase | Upskilling | Partners and contractors | Hiring |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tactical | Best effort, from free resources | Do essential work with wide privileged access; first point of escalation | No new cloud-experienced staff |
| Strategic | A learning plan, regular classes, budgeted certification, and a sandbox project for each person | Experts for the remaining gaps; second-tier escalation with moderated access | New roles for cloud-experienced people |
| Transformational | Continuous: training, hackathons, and tech talks | Staff augmentation with no privileged access | All roles reviewed for cloud-first work |

Source: [Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf).

Google Skills (formerly Google Cloud Skills Boost) offers learning paths by role, hands-on labs on real cloud resources, and skill badges ([Training resources](https://cloud.google.com/learn/training), [Legal Change Log](https://cloud.google.com/legal-change-log)). Premium Support includes Google Skills training credits, and the TAM identifies learning opportunities ([Premium Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/premium)).

Skills also decide the migration order. Pick a first-mover team that is highly motivated, with clear goals and sponsors from business leadership ([Assess and discover your workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-assessing-and-discovering-your-workloads)). See [migration planning](note:1.4-migration-planning).

## Decision-making processes

Make each decision against explicit criteria and data, record it, and give it to the people closest to the work.

| Tool | Use it when |
|---|---|
| Architecture decision record (ADR) | Two or more engineering options exist, or no standard, blueprint, or documented solution covers the question |
| Vision statement with sign-off | Stakeholders have competing visions |
| POC with success criteria | You must choose between options or prove value |
| Error budget from the SLO | Reliability work competes with feature work |
| Migration criteria: business value, dependencies, teams, refactoring effort, compliance, availability | You plan migration batches |
| RACI matrix | Task ownership is unclear |

Sources: [Architecture decision records overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/architecture-decision-records), [Plan a hybrid and multicloud strategy](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/hybrid-multicloud-patterns/strategy), [Embracing risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/), [Assess and discover your workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-assessing-and-discovering-your-workloads).

**ADR details.** An ADR lists the authors, the context and problem, and the functional and non-functional requirements. It also lists the affected critical user journey, the key options, and the decision with its reasons. Add a timestamp. When you change a decision, keep the previous one and say why it changed. Store the ADR close to the code, ideally in the same version control system. Some teams mirror key decisions to a wiki for business owners ([Architecture decision records overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/architecture-decision-records)). This applies the core principle "Document your architecture" in [the Well-Architected Framework](note:1.2-well-architected-framework).

**Decision authority.** At transformational maturity, project teams can experiment without permission ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)). DORA research supports teams that choose their own tools and try ideas without outside approval ([DevOps capabilities](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/devops)).

## Customer success management

The exam guide does not define this term. For an architect, it means that the customer, internal or external, reaches the business result that justified the solution:

- Define success first. Set POC success criteria such as compatibility, downtime, and performance ([Best practices for validating a migration plan](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-best-practices)). Use SLOs as measurable goals ([Optimize your environment](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-optimizing-your-environment)). For KPIs and ROI, see [business requirements](note:1.1-business-requirements).
- Collect feedback from users, stakeholders, and team members, and use it to prioritize improvements ([Continuously improve and innovate](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/continuously-improve-and-innovate)).
- After a migration, update runbooks, support documents, and dashboards ([Best practices for validating a migration plan](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-google-cloud-best-practices)).

Cloud Customer Care offers four support services:

| Service | Designed for | Selected features |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | All customers | Documentation, community support, billing support, Active Assist recommendations |
| Standard | Small to medium organizations with workloads under development | Unlimited 1:1 technical support |
| Enhanced | Medium to large companies with production workloads | Faster response. Technical Account Advisor Service is an add-on. |
| Premium | Enterprises with priority workloads | Customer Aware Support, a named TAM, Operational Health Reviews, Event Management Service. Mission Critical Services is an add-on. |

Sources: [Get support with Cloud Customer Care](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/overview), [Premium Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/premium).

Google calls the TAM "the voice of customer health" ([Technical Account Management](https://cloud.google.com/consulting/portfolio/technical-account-management)). Premium Support features that appear in scenarios ([Premium Support overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/support/docs/premium)):

- **Customer Aware Support.** Customer Care keeps information about your architecture, partners, and projects, so that cases close faster.
- **Operational Health Reviews.** Regular reviews of progress, blockers, and support and spend trends.
- **Event Management Service.** Preparation for planned peak events, such as a product launch or a major sale. Requests need 30 days of lead time.
- **Mission Critical Services.** Assessment and mitigation of possible disruptions for essential environments.

For support of a running solution, see [supporting deployed solutions](note:6.4-support).

## Business continuity

DR is a subset of business continuity planning. DR planning starts with a business impact analysis that sets two key metrics: the RTO and the RPO. The DR guide also covers people and process ([Disaster recovery planning guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/dr-scenarios-planning-guide)):

- Make each task in the plan concrete. "Run the restore script" is too general.
- Make sure that users can access the DR environment before a disaster. Include them in regular DR tests.
- Train users to do their usual tasks in the DR environment.
- Test the plan regularly, and change it when a test finds an issue.

For DR patterns, see [disaster recovery](note:4.1-disaster-recovery). For backups, see [data protection](note:2.2-data-protection).

## Exam traps

- **"Migrate the most critical workload first to show value."** Choose a first mover that is not business critical, is typical, and has few dependencies. A mistake in a critical workload hurts the main line of business ([Assess and discover your workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/migration-to-gcp-assessing-and-discovering-your-workloads)).
- **A large change to a tool or process in one step.** Google recommends incremental change with early wins. This advice is not for reorganizations, where frequent changes reduce employee confidence ([Organizational change management in SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/organizational-change/)).
- **Partners own the platform for good.** Partners with wide privileged access as the first point of escalation is tactical maturity. Upskill the staff, and use partners for the remaining gaps ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)).
- **Skip the tactical phase.** The whitepaper warns against a leap to the strategic phase without production experience in the cloud ([Adoption Framework whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/google_cloud_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf)).
- **More approval as the safe answer.** DORA recommends peer review instead of heavyweight change approval ([DevOps capabilities](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/devops)).
- **Plan-Do-Check-Act for a reorganization.** The Deming cycle does not cover the human side of change ([Organizational change management in SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/organizational-change/)).
- **Training as the only fix.** People also need time and coaching, and the migration cost must be small ([Organizational change management in SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/organizational-change/)).
- **A decision record with only the final choice.** An ADR records the options and reasons, and keeps earlier decisions ([Architecture decision records overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/architecture-decision-records)).
