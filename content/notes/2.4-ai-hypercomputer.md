## Why it matters

Accelerators are scarce and expensive, so the way you get them matters as much as the type. The architect picks GPUs or TPUs and the platform that runs the job. The architect also picks the consumption model that gets the capacity at the right price and time. Exam questions give a job length, a start-date need, a fault tolerance, and a budget. You must map those to Spot VMs, Flex-start, calendar mode, reservations, or commitments.

## Key points

- AI Hypercomputer is Google Cloud's integrated system for AI and ML. It combines performance-optimized hardware, open software, and flexible consumption models ([AI Hypercomputer overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/overview)).
- Code that runs on TPUs must compile with the XLA compiler. Models with custom operations in the training loop fit GPUs better. Models with dynamic shapes are not well suited to TPUs ([Introduction to Cloud TPU](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/intro-to-tpu)).
- TPU7x (Ironwood) is the latest TPU available on Google Cloud. The legacy Cloud TPU API does not support TPU7x and later. Create these TPUs through Compute Engine or GKE instead ([TPU7x (Ironwood)](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu7x), [TPU resources in Compute Engine](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpus-in-compute-engine)).
- Clustered GPUs (A4X Max, A4X, A4, A3 Ultra, A3 Mega, A3 High 8-GPU) are for foundation-model training. General GPUs (G4, G2, A2, smaller A3 High) are for inference and small training ([Choose your accelerator infrastructure](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/choose-infrastructure), [About GPU accelerators](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/understand-gpus)).
- Flex-start (Dynamic Workload Scheduler) gives capacity for up to seven days to workloads that can wait for it. Google does not preempt Flex-start VMs ([Consumption options for AI Hypercomputer](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/consumption-models), [Schedule training jobs based on resource availability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/schedule-jobs-dws)).
- Future reservations in calendar mode reserve GPUs or TPUs from a date that you choose, for 1 to 90 days. You pay for the full period ([About future reservation requests in calendar mode](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/future-reservations-calendar-mode-overview), [Dynamic Workload Scheduler pricing](https://cloud.google.com/products/dws/pricing)).
- Spot VMs have the deepest discount, up to 91%, but Compute Engine can preempt them at any time, and no SLA covers them ([Spot VMs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/spot), [Consumption options for AI Hypercomputer](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/consumption-models)).
- Flexible committed use discounts cover only G2 and G4 GPUs. Resource-based commitments for any GPU need attached reservations ([Committed use discounts for Compute Engine](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/committed-use-discounts-overview), [Purchase commitments with attached reservations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/committed-use-discounts/purchase-commitments-with-reservations)).
- Multislice joins several TPU slices over the data-center network. Compute Engine does not support it. Google recommends GKE for Multislice ([TPU architecture](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/system-architecture-tpu-vm), [TPU resources in Compute Engine](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpus-in-compute-engine), [Cloud TPU Multislice Overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/multislice-introduction)).
- Cluster Director is a fully managed service that automates the lifecycle of Slurm clusters. It is GA ([Choose an orchestrator and deployment option](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/choose-platform), [Cluster Director is now generally available](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/cluster-director-is-now-generally-available)).
- Cloud Run offers NVIDIA L4 and RTX PRO 6000 GPUs with no reservation, and GPU services scale to zero ([GPU support for services](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/configuring/services/gpu)).

## What AI Hypercomputer is

AI Hypercomputer is the unified portfolio for Google Cloud's AI infrastructure. It gives one entry point to explore, configure, and deploy three components ([AI Hypercomputer overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/overview)):

| Component | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Performance-optimized infrastructure | NVIDIA GPUs, Cloud TPUs, networking, and storage |
| Open software | Optimized PyTorch, JAX, and TensorFlow, and orchestration platforms |
| Consumption options | Flex-start VMs, Spot VMs, reservations, and Dynamic Workload Scheduler |

Source: [AI Hypercomputer overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/overview).

Google integrates the three to maximize goodput, which is the measure of actual ML productivity ([AI Hypercomputer overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/overview)). You do not buy AI Hypercomputer as one product. You use its parts through Compute Engine, GKE, Cluster Director, and the storage and network services on this page. Agent Platform (formerly Vertex AI) uses the same GPUs and TPUs through its managed training and inference services ([Introduction to Cloud TPU](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/intro-to-tpu), [Schedule training jobs based on resource availability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/schedule-jobs-dws)).

## GPUs or TPUs

### Fit of the model

| Choose TPUs when | Choose GPUs when |
|---|---|
| Matrix computations dominate the model, and the main training loop has no custom operations | The model has many custom PyTorch or JAX operations that must run partly on CPUs |
| The model trains for weeks or months, with large effective batch sizes | The model uses TensorFlow operations that Cloud TPU does not support |
| The model has ultra-large embeddings, as in ranking and recommendation | The work needs frequent branching, high-precision arithmetic, or dynamic tensor shapes. Cloud TPUs are not suited to this work. |

Sources: [Introduction to Cloud TPU](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/intro-to-tpu), [TPU7x (Ironwood)](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu7x), [Training with TPU accelerators](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/training-with-tpu-vm).

TPU7x supports JAX and PyTorch, not TensorFlow. Starting with TPU v6e, TensorFlow is no longer supported ([TPU7x (Ironwood)](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu7x), [Training with TPU accelerators](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/training-with-tpu-vm)).

| Version | Positioning |
|---|---|

| v6e (Trillium) | Training, fine-tuning, and serving of transformer, text-to-image, and CNN models |

## Where to run the workload

| Platform | Choose it when | Avoid it when |
|---|---|---|
| Agent Platform serverless training and endpoints | The team focuses on MLOps and fast time to value, and wants no infrastructure to manage | You need full control of the stack or Slurm |
| Agent Platform training clusters | You need reserved accelerators with a Slurm interface, and Google manages the cluster. Access is through your sales representative. | The job is an experiment that does not need assured capacity |
| GKE | You need deep control, portability, and a custom high-performance AI platform | The team does not want to operate Kubernetes |
| Cloud Run | You serve smaller models or run event-driven inference that can scale to zero | You need more than one GPU per instance, or TPUs |
| Cluster Director | You want Slurm, and you want Google to automate the cluster lifecycle | You want Kubernetes tools |
| Cluster Toolkit | You want open-source blueprints for reproducible clusters and will operate them | You want a managed service |
| Compute Engine | You bring your own orchestrator or none. This is the least managed option. | You need Multislice |

## Consumption models

Match the consumption model to four facts in the scenario: job length, a fixed or flexible start, fault tolerance, and budget.

| Option | Use it when | Do not use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Spot VMs | The work is fault-tolerant and checkpointed, and the lowest price matters | You need an SLA, or the machine is A4X or bare metal |
| Flex-start (Dynamic Workload Scheduler) | The job runs 7 days or less and can wait for capacity. All nodes must start together. | The job needs more than 7 days or a fixed start, or the machine is A4X Max or A4X |
| Future reservation in calendar mode | The start date is fixed and the job runs 1 to 90 days on clustered GPUs or TPUs | The job needs more than 90 days, a GPU start less than 87 hours away, or may not use the full period |
| Future reservation in AI Hypercomputer (capacity blocks) | Long-running, large-scale training on clustered GPUs, starting up to one year ahead | The work is short or uncertain. You pay from the start time, used or not. |
| On-demand (standard) reservation | Critical general GPU work that needs very high assurance of capacity now | The machine is A4X Max, A4X, A4, or A3 Ultra |
| On-demand VMs | An urgent job with a flexible end | Scarce GPUs: on-demand capacity can stock out |

Sources: [Consumption options for AI Hypercomputer](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/consumption-models), [Compute Engine instances provisioning models](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/provisioning-models), [Choose a reservation type](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/choose-reservation-type), [Reserve capacity through your account team](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/reserve-capacity), [General GPUs overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/evaluate-general-gpus), [Plan your Cloud TPU resources](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/plan-tpus), [Spot VMs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/spot), [About future reservation requests in calendar mode](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/future-reservations-calendar-mode-overview), [AI Hypercomputer release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/release-notes), [Schedule training jobs based on resource availability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/schedule-jobs-dws).

### Flex-start compared with calendar mode

Dynamic Workload Scheduler has two modes. Flex-start improves obtainability and cost. Calendar mode gives predictable start times ([Introducing Dynamic Workload Scheduler](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/introducing-dynamic-workload-scheduler)).

| | Flex-start | Calendar mode |
|---|---|---|
| Start time | When capacity is available (best effort) | The date and time that you request |
| Duration | 10 minutes to 7 days | 1 to 90 days |
| Lead time | None | GPU requests start at least 87 hours after you submit them |
| Billing | For use, like on-demand, with discounts on some series | For the full reserved period, at a discount |
| Quota | Uses preemptible quota, but the VMs are not preempted | No quota for GPU and TPU requests |

Sources: [Compute Engine instances provisioning models](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/provisioning-models), [About Flex-start VMs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/about-flex-start-vms), [About future reservation requests in calendar mode](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/future-reservations-calendar-mode-overview), [Dynamic Workload Scheduler pricing](https://cloud.google.com/products/dws/pricing), [Schedule training jobs based on resource availability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/schedule-jobs-dws).

Google's own advice on the AI Hypercomputer strategy page:

- For a high level of assurance to get large clusters of accelerators, use a reservation.
- For fine-tuning workloads, use a future reservation in calendar mode.
- For inference, use either a long-running reservation or a future reservation in calendar mode.

Source: [Choose between general GPUs and clustered GPUs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/choose-strategy).

### Consumption on GKE and Agent Platform

- **GKE.** Flex-start with queued provisioning fits workloads that cannot start until all nodes are ready. Google strongly recommends Kueue with it for quota management. Dynamic Workload Scheduler on GKE does not use reservations ([About flex-start provisioning mode in GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/dws)). ComputeClasses define a priority list of fallback options. For example, GKE uses a reservation first, then flex-start, then on-demand capacity. ComputeClasses do not support future reservations or calendar mode ([About accelerator consumption options in GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/consumption-option)). On GKE, flex-start without queued provisioning is in Preview ([About flex-start provisioning mode in GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/dws)).
- **Agent Platform training.** Set `scheduling.strategy` to `FLEX_START` to use Dynamic Workload Scheduler for a serverless training job. The job timeout must be 7 days or less ([Schedule training jobs based on resource availability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/schedule-jobs-dws)). Spot VMs work only for serverless training and inference ([Use Spot VMs with inference](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/predictions/use-spot-vms)).
- **Agent Platform inference.** Flex-start VMs for inference are in Preview. After the requested time, up to 7 days, Agent Platform undeploys the model ([Use Flex-start VMs with inference](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/predictions/use-flex-start-vms)).

### Committed use discounts

- GPU commitments need reservations that you attach to the commitment ([Purchase commitments with attached reservations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/committed-use-discounts/purchase-commitments-with-reservations)).
- Compute flexible commitments cover GPUs only on G2 and G4, and never cover Spot VMs ([Committed use discounts for Compute Engine](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/committed-use-discounts-overview)).
- A4 instances get no sustained use discounts and no flexible committed use discounts ([GPU machines in the accelerator-optimized family](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/accelerator-optimized-machines)).
- A future reservation in AI Hypercomputer of one year or longer needs a resource-based commitment ([Consumption options for AI Hypercomputer](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/consumption-models)).

For commitments in general, see [Cost optimization and CapEx/OpEx](note:4.2-cost-optimization).

## Large-scale training

- **Slices and Multislice.** A slice is a set of TPU chips in one TPU Pod, joined by inter-chip interconnect (ICI). Multislice joins slices over the data-center network (DCN) ([TPU architecture](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/system-architecture-tpu-vm)). Use it when a job is too big for one slice ([Deploy TPU Multislices in GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/tpu-multislice)).
- **Pathways.** One JAX client can orchestrate work across several large TPU slices, up to thousands of chips ([Introduction to Pathways on Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/workloads/pathways-on-cloud/pathways-intro)).
- **Job orchestration on GKE.** JobSet manages a group of Kubernetes Jobs as one unit, which fits distributed training. Kueue queues batch and AI workloads ([Orchestrate Multislice workloads using JobSet and Kueue](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/tutorials/tpu-multislice-kueue), [Best practices for GKE AI/ML workload prioritization](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/best-practices/optimize-ai-utilization)).
- **Slurm.** Cluster Director uses a managed Slurm environment for fault-tolerant job scheduling ([Cluster Director overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/cluster-director/docs/overview)). It has no extra charge. You pay for compute, storage, and networking ([Cluster Director is now generally available](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/cluster-director-is-now-generally-available)).
- **Checkpoints.** Jobs on thousands of nodes can stop as often as every hour, so checkpoints are necessary ([Multi-Tier Checkpointing on GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/machine-learning/training/multi-tier-checkpointing)). Use Managed Lustre for synchronous checkpoints and latency-sensitive work. Use Cloud Storage for datasets, asynchronous checkpoints, and model weights. Turn on hierarchical namespace in Cloud Storage for fast data loads and frequent checkpoints ([About storage services for AI and ML workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/storage)).
- **Networking.** Clustered GPUs need a multi-VPC environment, whether you use Slurm, GKE, or Compute Engine ([GPU networking overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/networking-overview)).
- **AI zones.** These zones hold large GPU and TPU capacity. Run non-ML workloads in standard zones, because AI zones do not offer all Google Cloud services locally ([AI Zones](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/regions-zones/ai-zones)).

## Serving models

| Option | Choose it when | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud Run with GPUs | Smaller models, event-driven traffic, and scale to zero when idle | L4 or RTX PRO 6000 GPUs. One GPU per instance. Instance-based billing. No reservation. |
| GKE | Control and portability for large or many models | Inference Gateway adds optimized routing and load balancing for generative AI. Google strongly recommends Inference Quickstart for production. |
| Agent Platform endpoints | Managed serving of your own or open models | TPU serving on v5e, v6e, and TPU7x. Self-deploy fits predictable, high-volume traffic. |
| Model as a Service | You want no serving infrastructure | Pay-as-you-go fits variable, bursty traffic. See [Choosing AI and ML solutions](note:1.3-ai-solutions). |

Sources: [GPU support for services](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/configuring/services/gpu), [Introduction to AI/ML workloads on GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/machine-learning), [About GKE Inference Gateway](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/about-gke-inference-gateway), [About AI/ML model inference on GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/machine-learning/inference), [Deploy a model to Cloud TPU VMs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/predictions/use-tpu), [Open models overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/open-models/choose-serving-option).

Cloud Run GPU services keep zonal redundancy on by default. If you turn it off, the GPU second costs less, but failover capacity is not reserved ([GPU support for services](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/configuring/services/gpu)). Cloud Run jobs with GPUs also fit batch or offline inference ([Configure GPUs for Cloud Run jobs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/configuring/jobs/gpu)).

To load model weights on GKE, the inference guide gives three options:

- Cloud Storage FUSE for deployments with limited node scaling.
- Managed Lustre for consistent scale-out with low-latency access from many Pods.
- Hyperdisk ML for massive scale. One Hyperdisk ML volume attaches read-only to up to 2,500 instances.

Sources: [About AI/ML model inference on GKE](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/machine-learning/inference), [Hyperdisk overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/hyperdisks).

TPU inference needs v5e or newer, and Cloud TPU integrates with vLLM through the tpu-inference plugin ([Run inference on Cloud TPU](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu-inference)).

## Where Cloud Run functions fit

The exam guide lists Cloud Run functions (formerly Cloud Functions) next to AI Hypercomputer. In ML workflows, a function can act as the event trigger. The Agent Platform docs show a function that a Pub/Sub message triggers to start a pipeline run ([Trigger a pipeline run with Pub/Sub](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/pipelines/trigger-pubsub)). Cloud Run functions also run on Cloud Run infrastructure and can use its features, including NVIDIA GPUs ([Cloud Functions is now Cloud Run functions](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/serverless/google-cloud-functions-is-now-cloud-run-functions)). For heavy training, use Agent Platform, GKE, or Slurm. See [End-to-end ML workflows on Agent Platform](note:2.4-ml-workflows).

## Case study angles

[KnightMotives Automotive](case:knightmotives) says that its "current AI infrastructure is obsolete". It plans fully autonomous driving and needs a simulation environment and "a scalable AI/ML infrastructure". Map these needs as follows:

- Weeks-long training of large driving models on clustered GPUs fits a future reservation. Add a commitment if the use is steady ([Choose between general GPUs and clustered GPUs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/choose-strategy)).
- Short fine-tuning runs fit Flex-start or calendar mode ([Choose between general GPUs and clustered GPUs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/choose-strategy), [Consumption options for AI Hypercomputer](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/consumption-models)).
- Simulation jobs that restart from checkpoints can use Spot VMs for the lowest price ([Spot VMs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/spot), [Consumption options for AI Hypercomputer](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/consumption-models)).
- EU data protection applies to the autonomous platforms. Keep training data and accelerators in EU regions, and check which zones offer the GPU type. GPU availability varies by region and zone ([GPU locations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/regions-zones/gpu-regions-zones)).

## Exam traps

- **Flex-start is not Spot.** Flex-start VMs use preemptible quota, but Google does not preempt them ([Schedule training jobs based on resource availability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/machine-learning/training/schedule-jobs-dws)).
- **Flex-start for a fixed start date.** Flex-start is best effort. A job that must start on a known date needs calendar mode ([Consumption options for AI Hypercomputer](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/consumption-models), [Plan your Cloud TPU resources](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/plan-tpus)).
- **Calendar mode for a job that starts tomorrow.** GPU requests must start at least 87 hours after you submit them ([About future reservation requests in calendar mode](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/future-reservations-calendar-mode-overview)).
- **Calendar mode for more than 90 days.** Use a future reservation in AI Hypercomputer, which can start up to one year ahead ([Choose a reservation type](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/choose-reservation-type), [AI Hypercomputer release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/release-notes)).
- **Spot for a job that cannot restart.** Spot VMs have no SLA and a best-effort shutdown of up to 30 seconds ([Spot VMs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/spot)).
- **Flexible CUDs for H100 or B200 GPUs.** Flexible commitments cover only G2 and G4 GPUs ([Committed use discounts for Compute Engine](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/committed-use-discounts-overview)).
- **TensorFlow on TPU7x.** TPU7x supports JAX and PyTorch only ([TPU7x (Ironwood)](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu7x)).
- **Multislice on plain Compute Engine.** Compute Engine does not support Multislice. Use GKE ([TPU resources in Compute Engine](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpus-in-compute-engine)).
- **Parallelstore for new designs.** Cluster Toolkit deprecated its Parallelstore blueprints and its Parallelstore module ([Cluster Toolkit release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/cluster-toolkit/docs/release-notes)). For a parallel file system, use Managed Lustre ([About storage services for AI and ML workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ai-hypercomputer/docs/storage)).
