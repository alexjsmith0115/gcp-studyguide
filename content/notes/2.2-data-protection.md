## Why it matters

Replication keeps data available when a zone or a region fails. It also copies corruption and deletions to every replica. Each dataset therefore needs a backup or point-in-time recovery (PITR) method that meets its recovery point objective (RPO) and recovery time objective (RTO). The exam asks for the mechanism that restores the right data fast enough at the lowest cost. DR patterns are on the [Disaster recovery planning](note:4.1-disaster-recovery) page.

## Key points

- Replication does not protect against data corruption, user error, or malicious attacks ([Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection)).
- Compute Engine does not back up instances or disks unless you configure a backup or replication option. Google recommends hourly standard snapshots ([Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection)).
- A Cloud SQL PITR always creates a new instance. It typically gives an RPO of five minutes or less ([Perform point-in-time recovery (PITR)](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/pitr), [Restore an instance overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/restore)).
- Spanner PITR keeps old data versions for up to seven days. It does not protect against deletion of the database ([Point-in-time recovery (PITR) overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/pitr)).
- BigQuery time travel covers 2 to 7 days. Fail-safe keeps deleted data 7 more days, and only Cloud Customer Care can recover it ([Data retention with time travel and fail-safe](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/time-travel)).
- Bigtable, Firestore, AlloyDB, and Spanner restore a backup to a new table, database, or cluster, not over the existing one ([Bigtable backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigtable/docs/backups), [Back up and restore data](https://docs.cloud.google.com/firestore/native/docs/backups), [Data backup and recovery overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/backup/overview), [Restore overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/backup/restore-backup-overview)).
- A backup vault keeps backups immutable and indelible until the enforced retention ends ([Backup vaults for immutable and indelible backups](https://docs.cloud.google.com/backup-disaster-recovery/docs/concepts/backup-vault)).
- Cloud Storage soft delete keeps deleted objects and buckets for 7 days by default ([Soft delete overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/soft-delete)).

## Replication, backup, and PITR solve different problems

| Mechanism | Protects against | Does not protect against |
|---|---|---|
| Synchronous replication across zones | A zone outage, with no data loss | Corruption and deletion, which it copies |
| Asynchronous replication across regions | A region outage | Corruption and deletion; the most recent writes can be lost |
| Backups and snapshots | Deletion, corruption, and ransomware, if the copy is isolated | Changes after the last backup |
| PITR and time travel | Bad writes and logical corruption, to a chosen moment | Loss of the whole resource in some products, for example a Spanner database |

Sources: [Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection), [Point-in-time recovery (PITR) overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/pitr), [Data retention with time travel and fail-safe](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/time-travel).

Set the backup frequency from the RPO. Test restores, because the restore time is part of the RTO ([Perform testing for recovery from data loss](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/perform-testing-for-recovery-from-data-loss)).

## Compute Engine disks and instances

| Option | RPO | RTO | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard and archive snapshots | Minutes to hours | Minutes to hours | Geo-redundant disk backups for DR |
| Machine images | Minutes to hours | Minutes to hours | Configuration, metadata, permissions, and all attached disks of an instance |
| Backup and DR Service | Policy-defined | Minutes to hours | Immutable and indelible backups |
| Instant snapshots | Seconds to minutes | Seconds to minutes | Fast rollback after a user error or failed upgrade |
| Disk clones | Zero | Seconds to minutes | Staging copies and backup checks |
| Synchronous replication (regional disks) | Zero | Seconds to minutes | Zone outage; not a backup |
| Asynchronous Replication | Seconds to minutes | Minutes | Region outage; not a backup |

Sources: [Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection).

- **Instant snapshots are not for DR.** They stay in the same location as the disk, and Compute Engine deletes them with the disk ([Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection)).
- **Snapshot location.** A multi-region location gives the highest availability. A regional location gives more control over where the data is ([About archive and standard disk snapshots](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/snapshots)).
- **Archive snapshots** fit backups that you keep for months or years and rarely read ([About archive and standard disk snapshots](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/snapshots)).
- **Snapshot schedules.** By default, scheduled snapshots stay after the source disk is deleted, unless you specify otherwise ([About snapshot schedules for disks](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/about-snapshot-schedules)).
- **Machine images** restore a whole instance with crash-consistent data from all Persistent Disk and Hyperdisk volumes ([Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection)).
- **Local SSD** cannot be backed up with snapshots, clones, machine images, or images ([About Local SSD disks](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/local-ssd)).

## Backup and DR Service

Backup and DR Service protects Compute Engine instances, disks, Filestore instances, Cloud SQL instances, and AlloyDB clusters with backup vaults ([Backup vaults for immutable and indelible backups](https://docs.cloud.google.com/backup-disaster-recovery/docs/concepts/backup-vault)). After the effective date of a locked retention, no one, not even a project owner, can decrease the retention period ([Backup vaults for immutable and indelible backups](https://docs.cloud.google.com/backup-disaster-recovery/docs/concepts/backup-vault)). A central administrator project can manage vaults and backup plans for many service projects ([Backup vaults for immutable and indelible backups](https://docs.cloud.google.com/backup-disaster-recovery/docs/concepts/backup-vault)).

Backup of MySQL and PostgreSQL databases in the legacy appliance management console is deprecated ([Backup and DR release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/backup-disaster-recovery/docs/release-notes)). Vaults, plans, and rules are described on the [Disaster recovery planning](note:4.1-disaster-recovery) page.

## Databases

| Product | Backups | Point-in-time recovery | Restore target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloud SQL | Automated and on-demand; automated retention of 1 to 365 days, or up to 10 years with enhanced backups | Log retention up to 35 days (Enterprise Plus) or 7 days (Enterprise) | A new instance for PITR |
| AlloyDB | Automated and on-demand, kept up to one year; default 14 days | Continuous backup: 14 days by default, 1 to 35 days | A new cluster |
| Spanner | Kept up to one year; scheduled full and incremental backups; copies to other regions or projects | 1 hour by default, up to 7 days | A new database with the same instance configuration |
| Bigtable | Standard or hot backups; automated daily backups kept 7 days by default | None | A new table in any instance, region, or project |
| Firestore | Daily or weekly schedules, kept up to 14 weeks | 7 days after you enable it, at minute granularity | A new database |
| Memorystore for Valkey | Automated (recommended) and on-demand | None | A new instance |

Sources: [Choose your backup option](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/backup-options), [Cloud SQL editions overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/editions-intro), [Data backup and recovery overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/backup/overview), [Backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/backup), [Point-in-time recovery (PITR) overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/pitr), [Bigtable backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigtable/docs/backups), [Back up and restore data](https://docs.cloud.google.com/firestore/native/docs/backups), [Point-in-time recovery (PITR) overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/firestore/native/docs/pitr), [About backups](https://docs.cloud.google.com/memorystore/docs/valkey/backups).

### Cloud SQL details

- If you do not choose a location, backups go to the multi-region nearest to the instance ([Choose your backup option](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/backup-options)).
- PITR is on by default for Enterprise Plus. For Enterprise, it is off by default when you create the instance with the gcloud CLI, Terraform, or the API ([Restore an instance overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/restore)).
- With standard backups, if you delete an instance without retained backups or a final backup, Cloud SQL deletes all its backups. A final backup is kept for 30 days by default, and you can keep it up to 365 days ([Choose your backup option](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/backup-options), [Cloud SQL backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/backups)).
- Enhanced backups are stored in a central backup project through Backup and DR Service, with enforced retention ([Cloud SQL backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/backups)).

### Spanner details

- Incremental backups need Enterprise edition or higher, and only a schedule can create them. A full backup schedule runs every 12 hours or more, and an incremental schedule every 4 hours or more ([Backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/backup)).
- A backup stays in the same instance, region, and project as its database. For compliance or business continuity, copy it to an instance in another region or project ([Backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/backup)).
- Deletion protection is off by default for a new database. Enable it on each database that you want to protect ([Prevent accidental database deletion](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/prevent-database-deletion)).
- For long-term backup or archiving past one year, export the database to a Cloud Storage bucket ([Spanner import and export overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/import-export-overview)).

### Bigtable details

- A hot backup is optimized for fast recovery. A standard backup fits long-term retention ([Bigtable backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigtable/docs/backups)).
- An on-demand backup can be kept up to 90 days in Enterprise edition or up to 365 days in Enterprise Plus. A copy of a backup is kept at most 30 days ([Bigtable backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigtable/docs/backups)).
- A backup does not include data that has not yet replicated from another cluster ([Bigtable backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigtable/docs/backups)).

## BigQuery

| Mechanism | Window | How you recover |
|---|---|---|
| Time travel | 2 to 7 days; the default is 7 | Query past data, or copy a table as it was at a point in time |
| Fail-safe | 7 days after the time travel window | Contact Cloud Customer Care |
| Table snapshot | Until the snapshot expires or you delete it | Restore a standard table from the read-only snapshot |
| Dataset undelete | The time travel window | `UNDROP SCHEMA` |
| Managed disaster recovery | RPO target of 15 minutes; RTO of 5 minutes after failover starts | Hard or soft failover to the secondary region (Enterprise Plus) |

Sources: [Data retention with time travel and fail-safe](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/time-travel), [Introduction to table snapshots](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/table-snapshots-intro), [Restore deleted tables](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/restore-deleted-tables), [Restore deleted datasets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/restore-deleted-datasets), [Managed disaster recovery](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/managed-disaster-recovery).

- A table snapshot stores only the bytes that differ from its base table ([Introduction to table snapshots](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/table-snapshots-intro)). A table clone is a writable copy, and you pay only for the changed data ([Introduction to table clones](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/table-clones-intro)).
- A hard failover does not wait for unreplicated data, so data loss can occur. A soft failover needs both regions to be available ([Managed disaster recovery](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/managed-disaster-recovery)).
- Cross-region dataset replication is not a DR plan by itself. In a total outage of the primary region, you cannot promote the secondary replica ([Cross-region dataset replication](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/data-replication)).
- A restore from time travel copies the data into a new table, but not the table partitioning. You cannot restore a single partition ([Access historical data](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/access-historical-data)).

## Cloud Storage and Filestore

- **Soft delete** is the recommended protection against accidental or malicious deletion. Object Versioning keeps older versions but does not protect against bucket deletion ([Object Versioning](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/object-versioning)).
- **Retention policies and holds** stop deletion. A retention policy works until the object reaches a set age. A hold works until you release it. A locked policy is irreversible ([Bucket Lock](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/bucket-lock), [Object holds](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/object-holds)). Configuration detail is on the [Configuring Cloud Storage](note:2.2-cloud-storage-config) page.
- **Filestore backups** can go to the same region or another region. You can restore a backup to a new instance in any supported location ([About backups](https://docs.cloud.google.com/filestore/docs/backups)).

## Map each mechanism to RPO and RTO

| Mechanism | RPO | RTO |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud Storage dual-region, default replication | 99.9% of new objects within 1 hour; 100% within 12 hours | Designed to be zero |
| Cloud Storage turbo replication | 15 minutes | Designed to be zero |
| Regional disk (regional Persistent Disk or Hyperdisk Balanced High Availability) | Zero for a zone outage | Force-attach takes less than one minute, plus instance and application recovery |
| Persistent Disk Asynchronous Replication | Target of one minute | Minutes |
| Standard snapshots | The snapshot interval (minutes to hours) | Minutes to hours |
| Cloud SQL HA | Zero for a zone outage | About 60 seconds until connections are reestablished |
| Cloud SQL PITR | Typically five minutes or less | Time to create a new instance |
| AlloyDB HA | Zero | Detection up to 30 seconds, then typically less than 30 seconds to start the standby |
| Spanner dual-region | Zero: a commit needs two replicas in each region | Usually within one minute after you start a failover; up to 45 minutes for a Google-managed failover |
| BigQuery managed disaster recovery | Target of 15 minutes | 5 minutes after you start the failover |
| Filestore instance replication | About 30 minutes | Manual: only the user can change the instance roles |

## Exam traps

- **Replication as the backup.** Replication copies corruption and user errors ([Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection)).
- **Instant snapshots for DR.** They live with the disk and are deleted with it ([Data protection options for disks and instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/data-protection)).
- **A Cloud SQL PITR over the existing instance.** PITR always creates a new instance ([Perform point-in-time recovery (PITR)](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/pitr)).
- **Restore from backups after you delete the Cloud SQL instance.** With standard backups and no retained or final backup, Cloud SQL deletes the backups ([Choose your backup option](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sql/docs/postgres/backup-recovery/backup-options)).
- **Spanner PITR to recover a dropped database.** PITR does not cover database deletion. Use backups and deletion protection ([Point-in-time recovery (PITR) overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/pitr), [Prevent accidental database deletion](https://docs.cloud.google.com/spanner/docs/prevent-database-deletion)).
- **Self-service restore from BigQuery fail-safe.** Only Cloud Customer Care can recover it ([Data retention with time travel and fail-safe](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/time-travel)).
- **A snapshot schedule for archive snapshots.** Schedules create standard snapshots ([About archive and standard disk snapshots](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/disks/snapshots)).
- **A Bigtable backup as a complete copy of a replicated instance.** Unreplicated data is not in the backup ([Bigtable backups overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigtable/docs/backups)).
