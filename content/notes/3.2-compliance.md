## Why it matters

Google gets its platform audited and signs contracts such as the HIPAA Business Associate Agreement (BAA). You design, configure, and prove the compliance of your own workload. Exam questions name a law, a standard, or an auditor request. Then they ask for the design that meets it with the smallest scope and the least custom work.

## Key points

- Compliance is a shared responsibility. The cloud provider always keeps responsibility for the network and infrastructure. You always keep responsibility for your access policies and data ([Shared responsibilities and shared fate on Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/shared-responsibility-shared-fate)).
- The US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) recognizes no HIPAA certification. Before you store protected health information (PHI), accept the Google Cloud BAA. Use only the products that it covers ([HIPAA Compliance on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/hipaa)).
- Every system that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data is in scope for PCI DSS. Systems that connect to them, or can affect their security, are in scope too ([Limiting scope of compliance for PCI environments in Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/limiting-compliance-scope-pci-environments-google-cloud)).
- Customer data is your data, not Google's. Google processes it only according to your agreements ([Privacy Resource Center](https://cloud.google.com/privacy)).
- Compliance Reports Manager gives you ISO/IEC certificates and SOC reports at no additional cost. Google Cloud issues only SOC 2 Type II reports ([Compliance Reports Manager](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/compliance-reports-manager), [SOC 2](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/soc-2)).
- The `gcp.resourceLocations` constraint limits where new resources are created. It does not move existing resources, and it does not affect global resources ([Restrict resource locations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/organization-policy/restrict-locations)).
- Assured Workloads applies a control package to a folder. Organization policy constraints and other features enforce its data location, personnel access, and key management controls ([Overview of Assured Workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/overview)).
- Access Transparency logs record the actions of Google personnel on your Customer Data. It is a default control for every organization, at no extra charge ([Enabling Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/enable), [Access Transparency pricing](https://cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/pricing)).
- Access Approval makes Google personnel get your explicit approval before they access Customer Data. It needs Access Transparency, and it makes support responses slower ([Overview of Access Approval](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-approval/docs/overview)).
- Key Access Justifications (KAJ) gives a reason for each use of an enrolled key. In some packages, a policy can deny the use. KAJ works only with Assured Workloads ([Overview of Key Access Justifications](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/key-access-justifications/docs/overview)).
- The `_Required` bucket keeps Admin Activity and System Event audit logs for 400 days. For longer retention, route logs to a log bucket with custom retention or to Cloud Storage, and lock it. You cannot undo a lock ([Store log entries](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/store-log-entries), [Configure log buckets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/buckets)).

## Shared responsibility

A Google audit report covers Google's part of the stack. It does not make your application compliant.

| Area | Google does | You do |
|---|---|---|
| Infrastructure | Secures the data centers, hardware, and network. Gets third-party audits such as SOC 2, ISO/IEC 27001, and PCI DSS. | Use services that are in scope for your standard. |
| Contracts | Signs a BAA for HIPAA. Commits in its contracts to comply with GDPR when it processes your personal data. | Accept the contracts that apply. Decide if you are a HIPAA covered entity or business associate. |
| Configuration | Gives secure defaults, blueprints, and guardrails. | Configure IAM, networks, encryption, and logging for each workload. |
| Data and access | Processes Customer Data only according to your agreements. | Control who can access the data and where it is stored. |

Sources: [Shared responsibilities and shared fate on Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/shared-responsibility-shared-fate), [HIPAA Compliance on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/hipaa), [PCI DSS](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/pci-dss), [GDPR and Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/privacy/gdpr), [Privacy Resource Center](https://cloud.google.com/privacy).

## Laws and regulations

| Law | Applies to | What Google Cloud gives you | What you must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| HIPAA (US health records) | Covered entities and their business associates | A BAA that covers the whole infrastructure and a list of products | Accept the BAA. Use only covered products for PHI. Do not use Pre-GA offerings with PHI unless their terms allow it. |
| COPPA (US, children under 13) | Operators of websites and online services for children under 13 | The COPPA page covers only Google Workspace for Education. | Meet COPPA as the operator of your own service. |
| GDPR (EU personal data) | Organizations in Europe or that serve users in Europe | Contract commitments, processing only on your instructions, and deletion within 180 days of a complete deletion instruction | Act as the data controller. Use the service features to answer data subject requests. |
| Data residency and sovereignty rules | Organizations that must keep data in a country or region | Location commitments for Customer Data at rest in supported services | Choose the regions. Enforce them with a constraint or with Assured Workloads. |

Sources: [HIPAA Compliance on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/hipaa), [COPPA (U.S.)](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/coppa), [GDPR and Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/privacy/gdpr), [Data residency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/data-residency).

Keep PHI and other personal data out of resource names, labels, and other metadata:

- Audit logs never include data contents, but they can capture resource metadata ([HIPAA Compliance on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/hipaa)).
- Residency commitments cover Customer Data at rest. Customer Data does not include resource identifiers, attributes, or data labels ([Data residency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/data-residency)).

## Data residency and sovereignty

| Requirement | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New resources must stay in approved locations | `gcp.resourceLocations` constraint with a value group, for example `in:eu-locations` | It blocks the creation of resources in other locations. It does not move existing resources. |
| Residency, support staff in one region, and violation alerts | Assured Workloads folder with a regional package, for example EU Data Boundary and Support | The package sets the location constraint and the support personnel controls. Assured Workloads reports violations. |
| A regulatory regime, for example FedRAMP High, IL5, ITAR, or CJIS | Assured Workloads regulatory data boundary | Predefined controls for the regime. Data Boundary for ITAR requires CMEK. |
| A local partner manages keys, access justifications, and audits | Sovereign Controls by Partners | Control packages that partners operate in Assured Workloads |
| A standalone cloud that a local partner operates | Google Cloud Dedicated | It is physically and logically isolated from the public cloud. It is available in France and in Germany (Preview). |
| Your own data center, disconnected from the internet | Google Distributed Cloud air-gapped | It runs independently, and Google cannot shut it down remotely. |

Sources: [Restrict resource locations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/organization-policy/restrict-locations), [Overview of Assured Workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/overview), [Control packages](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/control-packages), [Google Cloud Dedicated](https://cloud.google.com/products/dedicated), [Sovereign Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/sovereign-cloud).

Google groups its sovereign offerings as Google Cloud Data Boundary, Google Cloud Dedicated, and Google Distributed Cloud. Data Boundary uses Assured Workloads ([Sovereign Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/sovereign-cloud), [Data Boundary via Assured Workloads](https://cloud.google.com/security/products/assured-workloads)).

Facts about Assured Workloads:

- You need Cloud Identity, an organization, and the Assured Workloads Administrator role. If Access Transparency is off, it turns on when you create the folder ([Create a new Assured Workloads folder](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/create-folder)).
- Free tier packages include EU Data Boundary and US Data Boundary for Healthcare and Life Sciences. Premium packages add a percentage of the spend in the folder. The rate depends on the package, so see the cited page ([Assured Workloads pricing](https://cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/pricing)).
- Package names changed in June 2025. For example, EU Regions is now EU Data Boundary. Sovereign Controls for EU is now EU Data Boundary with Access Justifications ([Overview of Assured Workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/overview)).
- Assured Support limits your support cases to personnel who meet location and background conditions. It needs Enhanced Support or Premium Support ([Overview of Assured Workloads](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/docs/overview)).

## Control Google's access to your data

| Control | What it does | Requirements and cost |
|---|---|---|
| Access Transparency | Logs the actions of Google personnel on Customer Data. An entry shows the resource, the time, the reason, and the accessor's location and employer. | On by default for every organization. No extra charge. |
| Access Approval | Google personnel must get approval from your administrator first. You get an email or a Pub/Sub message, and you approve in the console or the API. | Access Transparency on. Set on a project, folder, or organization. Included with Standard, Enhanced, and Premium Support. |
| Key Access Justifications | Gives a justification for each operation on an enrolled key. In some packages, a policy approves or denies by justification code. | Only with Assured Workloads. The package sets the key types: Cloud EKM, Cloud HSM, or Cloud KMS software keys. |

Sources: [Overview of Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/overview), [Enabling Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/enable), [Access Transparency pricing](https://cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/pricing), [Overview of Access Approval](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-approval/docs/overview), [Access Approval pricing](https://cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-approval/pricing), [Overview of Key Access Justifications](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/key-access-justifications/docs/overview).

Design rules:

- Cloud Audit Logs record the actions of your own principals. Access Transparency logs record the actions of Google personnel. You need both for a full audit trail ([Overview of Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/overview)).
- Access Transparency does not log all access. If you give Google personnel access with your IAM policy, Cloud Audit Logs record their actions instead ([Access Transparency exclusions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/exclusions)).
- Access Approval can auto-approve a request for a time-sensitive outage. Auto-approval is off for EU Data Boundary with Access Justifications and for Sovereign Controls by Partners ([Overview of Access Approval](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-approval/docs/overview)).
- Access Transparency logs go to the `_Required` bucket. Give the Private Logs Viewer role to the people who must read them ([Store log entries](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/store-log-entries), [Route log entries](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/routing/overview), [Understanding and using Access Transparency logs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/reading-logs)).

For CMEK, Cloud HSM, and Cloud EKM, see [Encryption, Cloud KMS, and secrets](note:3.1-data-protection-kms).

## Card data and personal data

### PCI DSS scope reduction

| Strategy | How | Effect on scope |
|---|---|---|
| Resource hierarchy | Put all PCI projects in one folder. Give access only to designated users. | Other folders and projects can stay out of scope. |
| Network segmentation | Put the cardholder data environment (CDE) in a dedicated custom-mode VPC network with VPC Service Controls. Use constraints so that nobody can share the network. | Systems that cannot connect into the CDE can stay out of scope. |
| Tokenization | Replace the primary account number (PAN) with a token right after capture. Only designated services can get the PAN back. | A web application that uses only tokens can leave the set of connected-to systems. |

Sources: [Limiting scope of compliance for PCI environments in Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/limiting-compliance-scope-pci-environments-google-cloud).

- Google recommends all three strategies together. A system in the same subnet as a system that holds cardholder data stays in scope ([Limiting scope of compliance for PCI environments in Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/limiting-compliance-scope-pci-environments-google-cloud)).
- A Qualified Security Assessor (QSA) found a list of Google Cloud services compliant with PCI DSS 4.0.1. You stay responsible for your own application ([PCI DSS](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/pci-dss)).

### Personally identifiable information (PII)

- Sensitive Data Protection discovers sensitive data across an organization, folder, or project. It also inspects single resources and de-identifies data ([Sensitive Data Protection overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sensitive-data-protection/docs/sensitive-data-protection-overview)).
- Pseudonymization replaces sensitive values with cryptographic tokens. Format-preserving tokens can narrow the PCI DSS or HIPAA scope of downstream applications ([Pseudonymization](https://docs.cloud.google.com/sensitive-data-protection/docs/pseudonymization)).
- To record who reads PII, turn on Data Access audit logs. Resources open to `allUsers` or `allAuthenticatedUsers` do not generate audit logs ([Cloud Audit Logs overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/audit)).

For the de-identification methods, see [Securing AI workloads](note:3.1-securing-ai). For backups and retention of regulated data, see [Data protection: backup, recovery, and retention](note:2.2-data-protection).

## Certifications and audit reports

| Report | What it covers | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|
| SOC 1 | Internal controls that are relevant to financial reporting | Compliance Reports Manager |
| SOC 2 Type II | Controls for security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, or privacy over a period. Issued every quarter. | Compliance Reports Manager |
| SOC 3 | A public report with the same scope as the SOC 2 report | Compliance Reports Manager |
| ISO/IEC 27001 | The certified information security management system (ISMS) of Google Cloud | Compliance Reports Manager |
| PCI DSS | The services that a QSA reviewed | Compliance Reports Manager |

Sources: [SOC 1](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/soc-1), [SOC 2](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/soc-2), [SOC 3](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/soc-3), [ISO/IEC 27001](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/iso-27001), [PCI DSS](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/pci-dss), [Compliance Reports Manager](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/compliance-reports-manager).

- A SOC 2 Type I report covers the design of controls at one point in time. A Type II report also covers how well they operated over a period ([SOC 2](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/soc-2)).
- To share confidential resources from Compliance Reports Manager outside your organization, you need permission from your sales representative ([Compliance Reports Manager](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/compliance-reports-manager)).
- Audit Manager assesses your own workloads against a framework and collects evidence. SOC2 2017 is built in. Frameworks such as PCI DSS v4.0.1 need Security Command Center Premium or Assured Workloads ([Audit Manager overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/audit-manager/docs/overview)).

## Audit evidence and log retention

For log types and their defaults, see [Organization policy, VPC Service Controls, and audit logging](note:3.1-security-controls). This section is about how you keep the evidence.

| Audit need | Design | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One store for all projects | Aggregated sink on the organization or folder to a log bucket in a central project | An intercepting sink stops extra copies in the projects. |
| Keep logs for up to 10 years, with no changes | Log bucket with a retention of 1 to 3,650 days, then locked | The console cannot lock a bucket. Use gcloud. Add a lien to the project. |
| Keep logs for more than 10 years, or as files | Sink to a Cloud Storage bucket with a locked retention policy | Retention up to 100 years. The policy also applies to existing objects. |
| SQL analysis of logs | Observability Analytics (formerly Log Analytics) on upgraded log buckets, or a sink to BigQuery | Observability Analytics can also join log and trace data. |

Sources: [Aggregated sinks overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/export/aggregated_sinks_overview), [Quotas and limits](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/quotas), [Configure log buckets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/buckets), [Bucket Lock](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/bucket-lock), [Analyze logs using Logs Explorer and Observability Analytics](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/log-analytics).

Design rules:

- For HIPAA, Google recommends that you export audit logs to Cloud Storage for archives and to BigQuery for analysis ([HIPAA Compliance on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/hipaa)).
- A sink routes only the log entries that arrive after you create it. You can copy older entries from a log bucket to Cloud Storage ([Route log entries](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/routing/overview)).
- A longer log bucket retention applies only from now on. Logs cannot be recovered after their retention period ends ([Configure log buckets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/buckets)).
- Locking a retention policy is irreversible. After the lock, you can increase the period, but you cannot reduce or remove it ([Bucket Lock](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/bucket-lock)).

## Exam traps

- **"Google Cloud is HIPAA certified, so the workload is compliant."** HHS recognizes no HIPAA certification, and compliance is shared. Accept the BAA and configure the workload ([HIPAA Compliance on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/hipaa)).
- **"After you accept the BAA, any product can hold PHI."** Use only covered products. Do not use Pre-GA offerings with PHI unless their terms allow it ([HIPAA Compliance on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/hipaa)).
- **"Firewall rules take the web tier out of PCI scope."** A system in the same subnet as the CDE stays in scope. Use a separate network and tokens ([Limiting scope of compliance for PCI environments in Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/limiting-compliance-scope-pci-environments-google-cloud)).
- **"The location constraint moved our old US buckets to the EU."** It applies only to new resources. Move the existing data yourself ([Restrict resource locations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/organization-policy/restrict-locations)).
- **"Buy Premium Support to turn on Access Transparency."** It is a default control for every organization, at no extra charge ([Enabling Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/enable), [Access Transparency pricing](https://cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/pricing)).
- **"Access Transparency logs show what our own administrators did."** They show the actions of Google personnel. Cloud Audit Logs show the actions of your principals ([Overview of Access Transparency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/access-transparency/docs/overview)).
- **"Turn on Key Access Justifications for the keys in an ordinary project."** KAJ works only with Assured Workloads ([Overview of Key Access Justifications](https://docs.cloud.google.com/assured-workloads/key-access-justifications/docs/overview)).
- **"Create a sink now to export last year's logs."** A sink does not route older entries. Copy them from the log bucket ([Route log entries](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/routing/overview)).
