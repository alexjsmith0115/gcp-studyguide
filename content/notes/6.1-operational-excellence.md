## Why it matters

Objective 6.1 tests one document set: the operational excellence pillar of the Google Cloud Well-Architected Framework. The exam gives you an operations problem and asks which practice the pillar recommends. The pillar is organized as five principles, and each principle has a short list of recommendations. If you know the principle names and the recommendations under each, you can match most scenarios to the recommended answer. The framework as a whole is in [The Google Cloud Well-Architected Framework](note:1.2-well-architected-framework).

## Key points

- The pillar has five core principles: ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps; manage incidents and problems; manage and optimize cloud resources; automate and manage change; continuously improve and innovate ([Operational excellence pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence)).
- The pillar says to use automation, orchestration, and data-driven insights. Automation removes toil, orchestration coordinates complex processes, and data gives evidence for decisions ([Operational excellence pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence)).
- Operational readiness has four focus areas: workforce, processes, tooling, and governance. Each recommendation maps to one or more of them ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).
- The cloud operations team must define SLOs and SLAs for all critical workloads. SLOs must be SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).
- Capacity planning must include quotas, seasonal peaks, planned events, and capacity for graceful failover to the DR system ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).
- Incident response needs defined roles (for example, incident commanders and communicators), escalation paths, and a runbook that you review and update regularly ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)).
- A post-incident review (postmortem) must find the immediate cause and the systemic issues, and it must be blameless ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)).
- Resource management in this pillar covers right-sizing (Cloud Monitoring and Recommender), autoscaling, discounts, labels for cost tracking, and budgets ([Manage and optimize cloud resources](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-and-optimize-cloud-resources)).
- Change management rests on IaC (for example, Terraform), version control, CI/CD with Cloud Build and Cloud Deploy, configuration management tools, and automated tests ([Automate and manage change](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/automate-and-manage-change)).
- Continuous improvement uses a blameless learning culture, retrospectives (the Start-Stop-Continue model), feedback, and KPIs that you track in Cloud Monitoring and BigQuery ([Continuously improve and innovate](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/continuously-improve-and-innovate)).

## Operational readiness: the four focus areas

The pillar defines operational readiness as how your organization prepares to operate workloads on Google Cloud, for go-live and for day-2 operations. It calls these operations "CloudOps" ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).

| Focus area | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Workforce | Roles and responsibilities, skills, a learning program, team structure, and hiring. |
| Processes | Observability, managing service disruptions, cloud delivery, and core cloud operations. |
| Tooling | The tools that support CloudOps processes. |
| Governance | Service levels and reporting, cloud financials, the cloud operating model, architecture review boards, and compliance. |

Source: [Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops).

Use the focus areas to find gaps. For example, a team with good tools but no named incident roles has a workforce gap. The workforce focus area includes clear roles and responsibilities ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).

## Principle 1: operational readiness and performance

**SLOs and SLAs.** An SLO is a target for the level of service and performance, such as "99.9% availability" or "average response time less than 200 ms". An SLA is a contract with customers that includes penalties or remedies. Cloud Monitoring and SLIs let you define and track SLOs ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)). SLO mechanics (error budgets, burn-rate alerts) are in [Quality control measures](note:6.5-quality-control) and [Google Cloud Observability](note:6.2-observability).

**Comprehensive observability.** The pillar recommends Google Cloud Observability (formerly the Cloud operations suite, or Stackdriver) plus third-party tools where they add value. Monitor system health metrics (CPU, memory, network, disk I/O, response time) and business-specific metrics, and set alerts that notify teams before users see a problem ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).

**Performance and load testing.** Load testing simulates realistic traffic. Stress testing pushes the system to its limits to find bottlenecks. Use the results to change resource allocation, configuration, or caching ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)). See [Reliability in production](note:6.6-production-reliability).

**Capacity planning.** Analyze historical usage, growth projections, and business requirements. Include quotas. Plan for seasonal peaks, product launches, and capacity that supports graceful failover to the DR system during a disaster ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).

**Continuous monitoring and optimization.** Review logs and traces regularly. Use caching, database optimization, and code profiling to improve performance ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).

## Principle 2: incidents and problems

The pillar lists four foundational elements for incident and problem management: continuous monitoring, automation, orchestration, and data-driven insights. It recommends the Google SRE book as supplemental reading ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)).

| Recommendation | What the docs require |
|---|---|
| Clear incident response procedures | Define roles such as incident commanders, investigators, communicators, and technical experts. Define communication channels and escalation paths. Put the procedure in a runbook or playbook that covers communication, triage, investigation, and resolution. Review the runbook regularly. |
| Centralized incident management | One system for incident data, task assignment, and communication. It gives visibility, coordination, and accountability. It integrates with monitoring and ticketing tools. |
| Post-incident reviews | A multidisciplinary team builds a timeline and finds the root cause, the systemic issues, and the contributing factors. The report goes to all stakeholders. The culture must be blameless. |
| Knowledge base | Known issues, solutions, and troubleshooting guides. Make it easy to access, and update it from incident trends. |
| Automated incident response | Use products such as Cloud Run functions (formerly Cloud Functions) or Cloud Run to detect problems and run predefined remediation, such as a rollback to a known good state. |

Source: [Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems).

The incident roles and the postmortem process from the SRE books are in [Supporting deployed solutions](note:6.4-support) and [Testing, validation, and root cause analysis](note:4.1-testing-and-troubleshooting).

## Principle 3: manage and optimize cloud resources

This principle is in the operational excellence pillar, not only in the cost optimization pillar. The docs say its goals are efficiency, performance, and scalability, not only lower cost ([Manage and optimize cloud resources](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-and-optimize-cloud-resources)).

| Recommendation | Google Cloud tools named in the docs |
|---|---|
| Right-size resources | Cloud Monitoring for utilization; Recommender for right-sizing recommendations; custom metrics and alerts to trigger action. |
| Use autoscaling | MIG autoscaling policies; GKE cluster autoscaler, Horizontal Pod Autoscaler, Vertical Pod Autoscaler, node auto-provisioning; Cloud Run built-in autoscaling. |
| Leverage cost optimization strategies | Committed use discounts, sustained use discounts, Spot VMs, budgets and alerts. Review pricing models regularly. |
| Track resource usage and costs | Labels and tags; Cloud Billing reports and custom dashboards; cost postmortems. |
| Cost allocation and budgeting | Chargeback to teams; Cloud Billing budgets and alerts. |

Source: [Manage and optimize cloud resources](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-and-optimize-cloud-resources).

Details on discounts and FinOps are in [Cost optimization and CapEx/OpEx](note:4.2-cost-optimization).

## Principle 4: automate and manage change

The docs give four foundational elements of change management: change governance, risk assessment, testing and validation, and controlled deployment with a way to roll back ([Automate and manage change](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/automate-and-manage-change)).

| Recommendation | Why the pillar recommends it |
|---|---|
| Adopt IaC | Declarative, human-readable configuration gives consistency, repeatability, accountability, and easy rollback. The docs name Terraform. |
| Implement version control | Git tracks who changed what and when, and lets you roll back. |
| Build CI/CD pipelines | Cloud Build runs build steps from triggers such as pushes and pull requests. Cloud Deploy promotes releases through test, staging, and production, with traffic splitting and rollback. |
| Use configuration management tools | Puppet, Chef, Ansible, and VM Manager apply configuration consistently and reduce manual errors. |
| Automate testing | Unit, integration, and end-to-end tests in the pipeline find defects before production. Define pass and fail criteria. |

Source: [Automate and manage change](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/automate-and-manage-change).

The implementation details are in [SDLC, CI/CD, and service provisioning](note:4.1-sdlc-cicd) and [Deployment and release management](note:6.3-release-management).

## Principle 5: continuously improve and innovate

This principle maps mainly to the workforce focus area ([Continuously improve and innovate](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/continuously-improve-and-innovate)).

- **Culture of learning.** Encourage experiments and knowledge sharing. Treat failures as chances to learn, in a blameless culture.
- **Regular retrospectives.** Hold them after projects or major incidents. The docs suggest the Start-Stop-Continue model: start new practices, stop practices that do not help, and continue practices that work.
- **Stay up to date.** Use training, certifications, conferences, and webinars.
- **Seek feedback.** Use surveys, user interviews, in-product feedback forms, and team meetings. Analyze the feedback for themes before you prioritize work.
- **Measure progress.** KPIs reflect overall performance. Metrics are the data points that feed KPIs. Collect data with Cloud Monitoring, store and analyze it in BigQuery, and show it on dashboards.

Source: [Continuously improve and innovate](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/continuously-improve-and-innovate).

## How to answer 6.1 questions

1. Find the symptom in the stem: missing targets, slow incident response, repeated incidents, waste, drift, or no learning.
2. Map the symptom to a principle with the table above.
3. Prefer the answer that automates and measures. The pillar says to embrace automation, orchestration, and data-driven insights. They reduce manual labor and support evidence-based decisions ([Operational excellence pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence)).
4. Reject answers that punish people. The pillar requires a blameless culture for reviews and learning ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)).

## Exam traps

- **A manual checklist to stop drift.** When environments differ because people change them by hand, the answer is IaC in version control, deployed by a pipeline. A better checklist is still manual ([Automate and manage change](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/automate-and-manage-change)).
- **Blame after an outage.** An option that removes an engineer's access or disciplines a person is wrong. The pillar requires blameless post-incident reviews that look for systemic issues ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)).
- **A review that stops at the immediate cause.** The docs require the review to find the immediate cause and the systemic issues and contributing factors, such as missing training or communication breakdowns ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)).
- **Vague SLOs.** "The site must be fast" is not an SLO. An SLO must be SMART, for example "average response time less than 200 ms" ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).
- **Capacity plans that ignore failover.** A plan sized only for normal peaks fails the pillar's guidance. It must also support graceful failover to the DR system ([Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops)).
- **A runbook written once.** The docs say to review and update runbooks and the knowledge base regularly ([Manage incidents and problems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-incidents-and-problems)).
- **Cost is "only a cost pillar topic".** Right-sizing, autoscaling, and budgets are also recommendations in the operational excellence pillar ([Manage and optimize cloud resources](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/manage-and-optimize-cloud-resources)).
