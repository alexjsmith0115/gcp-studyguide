## Why it matters

The Google Cloud Well-Architected Framework is Google's set of principles and recommendations for cloud designs that are secure, efficient, resilient, high-performing, cost-effective, and sustainable ([Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework)). The exam guide names it in objective 1.2, but its principles decide answers in every domain. In a scenario, two options often both work. The requirement words point to one principle, and that principle selects the Google-recommended option.

## Key points

- The framework has six pillars: operational excellence; security, privacy, and compliance; reliability; cost optimization; performance optimization; and sustainability ([Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework)).
- A pillar covers one non-functional focus area. A perspective is a cross-pillar view for a technology, domain, or sector. Two perspectives exist: AI and ML, and financial services (FS) ([Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework)).
- Five core principles come before the pillars: design for change, document your architecture, simplify your design and use fully managed services, decouple your architecture, and use a stateless architecture ([Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework)).
- Sustainability became a full pillar on January 28, 2026. Google removed the old System Design category on August 27, 2024 ([What's new in the Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/whats-new)).
- Design a system to be "just reliable enough for user happiness". A 100% reliability goal is often not the most effective strategy ([Set realistic targets for reliability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/set-targets)).
- In the cloud, most costs are OpEx and occur when you consume resources. On-premises assets are acquired, and their cost is depreciated over their operating life ([Cost optimization pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization)).
- Performance optimization is a continuous cycle: define requirements, design and deploy, monitor and analyze, and optimize ([Performance optimization pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization)).
- Security is a shared responsibility between you and Google. Under the shared fate model, Google also gives best-practice guidance and secured, attested infrastructure code ([Security pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security), [Shared responsibilities and shared fate](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/shared-responsibility-shared-fate)).
- Sustainability is also shared. Google is responsible for the sustainability of Google Cloud. You are responsible for sustainability in the cloud ([Sustainability pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability)).

## How the framework is organized

| Part | What it contains | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Core principles | Five principles that apply to every pillar | You make the first design choices, before you look at one pillar |
| Pillars | Six non-functional focus areas. Each pillar has core principles, and each principle has recommendations | The stem states one requirement type, for example cost or availability |
| Perspectives | AI and ML, and FS. Each perspective has one page for each of five pillars (no sustainability page) | The workload is an AI or ML system, or a regulated financial service |

Sources: [Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework), [AI and ML perspective](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/ai-ml), [FS perspective](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/fsi).

Perspective recommendations align with the pillars. For example, the FS perspective recommends a DR strategy that meets data residency rules, because residency limits the choice of failover region ([Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework)). AI energy efficiency is a sustainability pillar principle, not a perspective page ([Sustainability pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability)).

## Core principles

| Principle | What it means for a design | Signal in the stem |
|---|---|---|
| Design for change | Deliver small changes often and get fast feedback. Track the DORA software delivery metrics. | "Release faster", "reduce deployment risk" |
| Document your architecture | Keep clear, current documentation and a change history, so that teams share context for later decisions. | "New architects join", "teams repeat old decisions" |
| Simplify your design and use fully managed services | Use managed services where you can. Start with a minimal viable product (MVP). Do not over-engineer. | "Least operational overhead", "small team" |
| Decouple your architecture | Split the system into components that run independently. You can then upgrade, secure, monitor, and scale each part on its own. | "Scale one part", "release parts independently" |
| Use a stateless architecture | Keep no local state. Use shared storage or cached services. Stateless applications scale fast and survive hard restarts. | "Autoscale", "replace instances at any time" |

Source: [Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework).

## Pillar principles

### Operational excellence

For detail, see [The operational excellence pillar](note:6.1-operational-excellence).

| Principle | Design decision it drives |
|---|---|
| Ensure operational readiness and performance using CloudOps | Define SMART SLOs and SLAs. Monitor, load test, and plan capacity. |
| Manage incidents and problems | Use clear incident response procedures, post-incident reviews, and preventive measures. |
| Manage and optimize cloud resources | Right-size, autoscale, and monitor cost. |
| Automate and manage change | Use IaC (for example, Terraform), version control, CI/CD pipelines, and automated tests. |
| Continuously improve and innovate | Run retrospectives, learn, and act on feedback. |

Sources: [Operational excellence pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence), [CloudOps readiness](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/operational-readiness-and-performance-using-cloudops), [automate and manage change](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/automate-and-manage-change), [continuously improve and innovate](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/operational-excellence/continuously-improve-and-innovate).

### Security, privacy, and compliance

| Principle | Design decision it drives |
|---|---|
| Implement security by design | Add security from the first design phase. Use layered controls, hardened infrastructure such as Shielded VM, and encryption. Data at rest is encrypted by default; data in use is not. |
| Implement zero trust | Verify every request. Grant access on user identity and context with IAP and Chrome Enterprise Premium. Use VPC Service Controls perimeters to prevent data exfiltration. |
| Implement shift-left security | Add controls early in the SDLC: IaC, policy as code, Organization Policy Service, Artifact Analysis scans, and Binary Authorization. |
| Implement preemptive cyber defense | Act before attacks with threat intelligence and layered controls. |
| Use AI securely and responsibly | Follow the AI and ML perspective and Google's Secure AI Framework (SAIF). |
| Use AI for security | Use AI to detect threats and to automate security tasks. |
| Meet regulatory, compliance, and privacy needs | Use Assured Workloads, organization policies, and resource location policies for data residency. Monitor with Access Transparency and Key Access Justifications. |

Sources: [Security pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security), [security by design](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/implement-security-by-design), [zero trust](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/implement-zero-trust), [shift-left security](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/implement-shift-left-security), [AI for security](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/use-ai-for-security), [regulatory, compliance, and privacy needs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/meet-regulatory-compliance-and-privacy-needs).

### Reliability

Each reliability principle belongs to one focus area: scoping, observation, response, or learning.

| Principle | Focus area | Design decision it drives |
|---|---|---|
| Define reliability based on user-experience goals | Scoping | Measure what users see (success ratio, latency, errors), near the user. Do not rely only on server metrics such as CPU usage. |
| Set realistic targets for reliability | Scoping | Aim high (for example, 99.99%), but never 100%. The gap is the error budget. Weigh cost against the RTO and RPO. |
| Build highly available systems through resource redundancy | Scoping | Map failure domains. Replicate across zones and regions with automatic failover. Test the failover. |
| Take advantage of horizontal scalability | Scoping | Scale out with MIGs or Cloud Run. Vertical scaling has hard limits, and one large VM is a single point of failure. |
| Detect potential failures by using observability | Observation | Use metrics, logs, and traces. Focus alerts on critical metrics to limit alert fatigue. |
| Design for graceful degradation | Response | Throttle, drop excess requests early at the frontend, and handle partial errors and retries. |
| Perform testing for recovery from failures | Learning | Test regional failover, release rollbacks, and restores. Compare the results with the RTO and RPO. |
| Perform testing for recovery from data loss | Learning | Back up at least as often as the RPO. Restore backups in a non-production environment to prove them. |
| Conduct thorough postmortems | Learning | Write blameless reviews and share them widely. Avoid complex fixes for unlikely problems. |

Sources: [Reliability pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability) and its principle pages on [user-experience goals](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/define-reliability-based-on-user-experience-goals), [realistic targets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/set-targets), [redundancy](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/build-highly-available-systems), [horizontal scalability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/horizontal-scalability), [observability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/observability), [graceful degradation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/graceful-degradation), [recovery from failures](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/perform-testing-for-recovery-from-failures), [recovery from data loss](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/perform-testing-for-recovery-from-data-loss), and [postmortems](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/conduct-postmortems).

For detail, see [High availability, scalability, and performance](note:1.2-ha-scalability-performance), [Disaster recovery planning](note:4.1-disaster-recovery), and [Google Cloud Observability](note:6.2-observability).

### Cost optimization

| Principle | Design decision it drives |
|---|---|
| Align cloud spending with business value | Compare total cost of ownership (TCO), which includes management work, not only price. Prefer managed and serverless products, for example Cloud SQL instead of databases on VMs. Adopt FinOps. |
| Foster a culture of cost awareness | Give every role cost visibility. Use a standard set of labels, shared cost reports, and cost data in BigQuery. Know the discount options: CUDs, sustained use discounts, and Spot VMs. |
| Optimize resource usage | Match resources to each environment and workload. Use built-in features such as autoscaling and Autoclass. Set budgets and quotas, and restrict locations and products with organization policies. |
| Optimize continuously | Monitor business-relevant metrics. Act on Active Assist recommendations. Use sampling and aggregation to control observability cost. Alert first on issues that affect customers. |

Sources: [Cost optimization pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization), [business value](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/align-cloud-spending-business-value), [cost awareness](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/foster-culture-cost-awareness), [resource usage](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/optimize-resource-usage), [optimize continuously](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/optimize-continuously).

For detail, see [Cost optimization and CapEx/OpEx](note:4.2-cost-optimization).

### Performance optimization

| Principle | Design decision it drives |
|---|---|
| Plan resource allocation | Define performance requirements for each layer before design. Consider the deployment archetype, region, network, hosting, and storage. Monitor quotas and load test. |
| Take advantage of elasticity | Scale up before known peaks. Use autoscaling for unexpected surges. Evaluate predictive autoscaling on Compute Engine. Serverless products can scale to zero. |
| Promote modular design | Use loosely coupled, stateless modules with clear interfaces (APIs, message queues). Calls between modules add latency. |
| Continuously monitor and improve performance | Set SMART goals, collect KPIs, profile applications, and use tools such as Network Intelligence Center. |

Sources: [Performance optimization pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization), [resource allocation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/plan-resource-allocation), [elasticity](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/elasticity), [modular design](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/promote-modular-design), [monitor and improve](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/continuously-monitor-and-improve-performance).

### Sustainability

| Principle | Design decision it drives |
|---|---|
| Use regions that consume low-carbon energy | Compare regions by carbon-free energy (CFE%) and the Low CO2 indicator. Enforce low-carbon regions with the resource locations constraint. Run batch jobs in hours with cleaner power. |
| Optimize AI and ML workloads for energy efficiency | Use efficient models, TPUs, model compression, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning. |
| Optimize resource usage for sustainability | Autoscale, use Spot VMs for non-critical batch jobs, scale to zero, and avoid idle DR capacity. |
| Develop energy-efficient software | Write lean code, cache results, and choose efficient algorithms. |
| Optimize data and storage for sustainability | Remove duplicate and unused data, apply lifecycle policies, and minimize replication. |
| Continuously measure and improve sustainability | Use Carbon Footprint to set a baseline, find hotspots, and verify each optimization. |
| Promote a culture of sustainability | Train each role, and define carbon KPIs and non-functional requirements. |
| Align sustainability practices with industry guidelines | Follow recognized industry guidelines and frameworks. |

Sources: [Sustainability pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability), [low-carbon regions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/low-carbon-regions), [AI and ML energy efficiency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/ai-ml-energy-efficiency), [resource usage](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/optimize-resource-usage), [energy-efficient software](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/energy-efficient-software), [data and storage](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/optimize-storage), [measure and improve](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/continuously-measure-improve), [culture](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/culture).

## The perspectives in practice

The AI and ML perspective adds AI-specific principles to five pillars. Examples are "Use managed services and pre-trained models" (cost optimization), "Build an automated end-to-end MLOps platform" (reliability), and "Verify and protect inputs" (security) ([cost](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/ai-ml/cost-optimization), [reliability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/ai-ml/reliability), [security](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/ai-ml/security)). For AI design choices, see [Choosing AI and ML solutions](note:1.3-ai-solutions) and [Securing AI workloads](note:3.1-securing-ai).

The FS reliability page gives clear availability rules ([FS perspective: Reliability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/fsi/reliability)):

- For critical FS applications, use at least two regions, with three zones in each region.
- Prefer resources with a wider location scope: regional instead of zonal, and multi-regional or global instead of regional.
- The availabilities of chained tiers multiply: four tiers at 99.9% give 99.6%.
- Parallel services give a higher availability than one service: 1 − (1 − A) × (1 − B). Two 99% services give 98% in a chain but 99.99% in parallel.

## Trade-offs between pillars

| Tension | What the framework says |
|---|---|
| Reliability and cost | Higher reliability can cost much more. Set a target from a cost-benefit analysis. |
| Performance and cost | Performance work can cost more, but autoscaling can improve both. |
| Modularity and latency | Calls between modules add latency. When performance is critical, tighter coupling might be appropriate. |
| Security and performance | Plan security features early, and accept their performance cost. |
| Sustainability and DR | Idle standby capacity wastes energy. Prefer right-sized active-active, or provision the DR stack with IaC on demand within the RTO. |
| Cost, latency, and carbon | The cheapest region might not meet latency or sustainability goals. Compare regions with the Region Picker. |
| Observability and cost | Collect enough data to troubleshoot. Limit cost with sampling, aggregation, and log exclusion filters. |
| Sustainability and cost | These goals usually align: rightsizing, autoscaling, and removing idle resources reduce both. |

Sources: [realistic targets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/set-targets), [performance pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization), [modular design](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/promote-modular-design), [resource allocation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/plan-resource-allocation), [sustainable resource usage](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/optimize-resource-usage), [cost-optimized resource usage](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/optimize-resource-usage), [optimize continuously](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/optimize-continuously), [observability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/observability), [sustainability pillar](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability).

## From scenario words to the deciding principle

| Words in the stem | Pillar: principle | Answer pattern that usually wins |
|---|---|---|
| "Least operational overhead", "small team" | Core: simplify and use managed services. Cost: align spending with business value | A managed or serverless product (Cloud SQL, GKE Autopilot, Cloud Run) |
| "Users are satisfied", "cost-sensitive", "100%" | Reliability: set realistic targets | An SLO at the level users need, and an error budget |
| "Measure reliability", "SLI" | Reliability: user-experience goals | Request success ratio and latency near the user, not CPU |
| "Zone outage", "single point of failure" | Reliability: resource redundancy | Regional (multi-zone) resources with automatic failover |
| "Demand exceeds capacity", "cascading failure" | Reliability: graceful degradation | Throttle, drop excess requests at the frontend, and handle partial errors and retries |
| "RPO of N minutes", "prove the backups" | Reliability: recovery from data loss | Backups at least every N minutes; restore tests |
| "Known sale date", "annual event" | Performance: elasticity | Scale out before the event, and keep autoscaling for surprises |
| "Scale one component", "monolith" | Core: decouple. Performance: modular design | Services with clear interfaces |
| "Chargeback", "cost per team" | Cost: culture of cost awareness | Standard labels, cost data in BigQuery, and shared reports |
| "Idle", "overprovisioned" | Cost: optimize continuously | Active Assist recommendations |
| "Data residency", "regulated" | Security: regulatory, compliance, and privacy needs | Resource location policies, Assured Workloads |
| "Replace the VPN", "device posture" | Security: zero trust | IAP with Chrome Enterprise Premium |
| "Carbon", "clean energy" | Sustainability: low-carbon regions; measure and improve | Low-carbon regions enforced by policy; Carbon Footprint |

Sources: the principle pages cited above, mainly the [reliability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability), [cost](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization), and [security](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security) pillars, [elasticity](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/elasticity), and [low-carbon regions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/low-carbon-regions).

## Exam traps

- **Old framework names.** Older material says Architecture Framework and lists a System Design category. Google removed that category in 2024, and sustainability is now a pillar ([What's new in the Well-Architected Framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/whats-new)).
- **Extra nines for their own sake.** An option that adds regions when users are already satisfied is usually wrong ([Set realistic targets for reliability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/set-targets)).
- **Server metrics as reliability.** High CPU is not an outage if users are not affected. Choose user-facing indicators ([Define reliability based on user-experience goals](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/define-reliability-based-on-user-experience-goals)).
- **A circuit breaker for overload.** A circuit breaker drops all traffic. The framework prefers to drop only the excess requests early, so most users still get service ([Design for graceful degradation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/graceful-degradation)).
- **Autoscaling alone for a known event.** Reactive autoscaling can take several minutes. Scale out before a known peak. Predictive autoscaling does not forecast annual or one-time events ([Take advantage of elasticity](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/performance-optimization/elasticity), [Scaling based on predictions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/autoscaler/predictive-autoscaling)).
- **Price instead of TCO.** Self-managed VMs add patching and scaling work to the TCO. When the stem mentions team effort, a managed service usually wins ([Align cloud spending with business value](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/cost-optimization/align-cloud-spending-business-value)).
- **Security by design and shift-left mixed up.** Security by design prevents fundamental design flaws. Shift-left security prevents implementation defects, such as bugs and misconfigurations ([Implement shift-left security](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/implement-shift-left-security)).
- **A chain of good SLAs.** Four chained tiers at 99.9% give about 99.6%, which is lower than any one tier ([FS perspective: Reliability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/perspectives/fsi/reliability)).
- **Idle DR as the default.** An idle warm standby wastes energy. When the RTO allows, provision the DR stack with IaC when you need it ([Optimize resource usage for sustainability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/sustainability/optimize-resource-usage)).
